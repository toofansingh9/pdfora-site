PDFs are built to be read, not rewritten. When a client sends over a contract that needs three clauses changed, or you find an old report whose source file vanished years ago, retyping the whole thing is the last thing you want to do. PDFora's PDF to Word converter turns your PDF into an editable DOCX file, keeping the layout, fonts, tables, and images as close to the original as possible.

The conversion runs on PDFora's secure cloud engine. Your file travels over an encrypted connection, gets converted on the server, and is automatically deleted once processing finishes — nothing sits in a library or gets reused. While most PDFora tools work entirely inside your browser, format conversions like PDF to DOCX need heavier processing, so this one uses that encrypted pipeline instead. Either way, your document stays yours.

Everything here is free: no signup, no watermark, no page limit pushing you toward a paid plan. Upload a PDF, download a Word document, and get back to editing. One thing to know upfront: scanned PDFs are pictures of text, so they convert as images unless you run OCR first.

How to convert PDF to Word online

  1. Open the PDF to Word tool on getpdfora.com — no account or installation needed.
  2. Click the upload area and pick your PDF, or drag the file straight onto the page.
  3. Wait a few seconds while the secure conversion engine rebuilds your document as an editable DOCX, matching fonts, tables, and layout as closely as it can.
  4. Click Download to save the Word file to your device.
  5. Open it in Microsoft Word, Google Docs, or LibreOffice and start editing.
  6. Skim the result once — dense layouts occasionally need a small nudge.

When to use this tool

  • Update a lease or service contract when the original Word file is long gone and only the signed PDF survives.
  • Pull quotes and paragraphs from a research paper into your literature review without retyping citations.
  • Rework last year's project proposal into this year's pitch instead of rebuilding the document from a blank page.
  • Fix a typo in a restaurant menu, flyer, or price list that a designer delivered as a locked PDF.
  • Translate a PDF manual by converting it to DOCX first, so translation tools and editors can work with real text.
  • Turn a PDF resume back into an editable document when you need to tailor it for a specific job posting.

Tips for the best results

  • If your PDF is a scan or a photo of a page, run it through an OCR tool first — otherwise the DOCX will contain images of text rather than words you can edit.
  • Documents built with common fonts like Arial, Calibri, or Times New Roman convert most faithfully; rare display fonts may be substituted with the closest match.
  • Multi-column newsletters and heavy design work convert best when you expect to spend a minute tidying text boxes afterwards.
  • Check tables after conversion. They usually arrive intact, but merged cells sometimes shift and are easy to fix in Word.
  • Converting a huge file? Split off just the pages you need first — smaller files convert faster and are easier to clean up.

Frequently asked questions

Will my converted Word document look exactly like the PDF?

Simple and moderately formatted documents usually come through nearly identical. Complex layouts — multiple columns, overlapping graphics, unusual fonts — may need small touch-ups after conversion. The engine preserves structure as closely as the PDF's internals allow.

Can I convert a scanned PDF to editable Word?

Not directly. A scanned PDF is essentially a photograph of a page, so it converts as images rather than editable text. Run OCR on the scan first, then convert the OCR'd PDF to DOCX.

Is the PDF to Word converter really free?

Yes. There is no signup, no watermark on the output, and no trial counter. You can convert as often as you need.

What happens to my file after conversion?

This tool uses PDFora's secure cloud engine: your PDF is sent over an encrypted connection, converted, and automatically deleted after processing. It is never stored long-term or shared.

Which programs can open the converted DOCX file?

Any modern word processor — Microsoft Word, Google Docs, LibreOffice Writer, and Apple Pages all open DOCX files. You can edit, reformat, and re-save the document freely.

Do tables and images survive the conversion?

Yes, tables are rebuilt as real Word tables and images are embedded in place. Very intricate tables with merged cells may need minor adjustment, but the data itself carries over.
