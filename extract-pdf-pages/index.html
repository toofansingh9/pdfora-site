Sometimes you don't want the whole document — you want three pages from the middle of it. PDFora's extract tool lets you pull selected pages out of any PDF and save them as a new file. Type something like 1-3, 5, 8 and you get exactly those pages, in that order, as one fresh PDF. The original stays untouched.

Extraction happens entirely on your device. The PDF is read and rebuilt by your browser, not by a remote server, so nothing you extract from — a signed agreement, a tax return, an appraisal — is ever transmitted anywhere. If you've hesitated to use online PDF tools with sensitive files, this is the workaround: the 'online' part is just the webpage, not your data.

There is no signup, no fee, and no watermark on the output. Extract two pages or two hundred, from one document or twenty, whenever you need. Nothing counts against a quota.

How to extract pages from a PDF online

  1. Open the Extract Pages tool on getpdfora.com in any modern browser.
  2. Add your PDF by clicking the picker or dragging the file onto the page.
  3. Type the pages you want in the selection box — single pages, ranges, or a mix, like 1-3, 5, 8.
  4. Double-check the numbers against the actual document; printed page numbers don't always match the PDF's page count.
  5. Click Extract and your browser assembles the chosen pages into a new PDF.
  6. Download the result. Your original file remains exactly as it was.

When to use this tool

  • Pull just the signature page from a long contract to send for countersigning.
  • Extract the executive summary from a 60-page report for people who won't read the rest.
  • Save only your own paper from a full journal issue or conference PDF.
  • Grab the relevant exhibit pages from a legal filing instead of forwarding the entire bundle.
  • Lift a single recipe, worksheet, or pattern out of a purchased e-book for printing.
  • Take the specifications pages out of a product manual to attach to a support ticket.

Tips for the best results

  • Mix ranges and single pages freely — 2, 5-9, 14 works exactly as you'd expect.
  • Pages appear in the new PDF in the order you list them, so write the selection in the order you want.
  • Open the PDF in a viewer first and note the viewer's page positions, since a cover page can shift everything by one.
  • If you want to remove a few pages rather than keep a few, the Delete Pages tool is the faster inverse.
  • Extract before you merge: pulling the good pages from several documents first makes for a much cleaner combined file.

Frequently asked questions

Can I extract non-consecutive pages from a PDF?

Yes. Separate them with commas — 1, 4, 9-11 pulls pages one, four, nine, ten, and eleven into a single new PDF. Any combination of single pages and ranges is fine.

Does extracting pages change my original PDF?

No. The tool reads your file and builds a brand-new PDF from the selected pages. The source document on your device is never modified.

Is it private enough for tax or legal documents?

Yes. The extraction runs locally in your browser and the file is never uploaded, so nobody — including PDFora — can see its contents. It's as private as opening the file on your own desktop.

Do the extracted pages keep their formatting?

They do. Pages are copied as-is, so fonts, images, links, and layout carry over exactly. Nothing is flattened or recompressed.

Can I extract pages from a password-protected PDF?

Not while it's locked — the browser can't read an encrypted file's pages. Remove the password first (you'll need to know it), then extract normally.

Is there a limit on how many pages I can extract?

No set limit and no charge. Since the work happens on your device, an enormous extraction from a very large file simply depends on your available memory, but typical jobs finish in seconds.
