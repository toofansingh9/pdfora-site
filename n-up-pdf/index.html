N-up (Pages per Sheet)

Place 2 or 4 pages of a PDF side by side on each landscape A4 sheet, so a long document prints on fewer pages.

Printing a 60-slide lecture deck one slide per page uses 60 sheets and gives you slides you could read from across the room. N-up printing puts several pages on one sheet instead. PDFora's N-up tool builds a new PDF where each sheet carries either two or four of your original pages, ready to print on any printer without fiddling with driver settings. Doing this in the PDF rather than in the print dialog has a practical advantage: the layout is fixed in the file, so the same handout prints identically on a home inkjet, an office copier or a print shop's machine, and you can check it on screen before a single sheet is used. It also means you can email the N-up version to someone else to print without explaining which driver settings to choose.

The layout is fixed and predictable. Every output sheet is A4 in landscape (842 by 595 points). In 2-up mode the sheet is split into two columns; in 4-up mode it is split into a 2 by 2 grid, filled left to right, top row first. Each original page is scaled down to fit its cell without distortion, shrunk to 95% of the cell so there is a small gap between pages, and centred.

The pages are not turned into pictures. pdf-lib embeds each source page into the new sheet as a vector object, so text and line drawings stay sharp when printed, even at a quarter of their original size. The work happens in your browser, and the download is named yourfile-nup.pdf. The scaling is worked out separately for each page, so a document that mixes portrait text pages and landscape charts still puts every page in its own cell at the largest size that fits.

How to put multiple PDF pages on one sheet

  1. Open the N-up tool and choose one PDF.
  2. Pick 2-up or 4-up under Pages per sheet. 2-up is the default.
  3. Click Create N-up. Long documents take a moment while every page is embedded.
  4. Open the result and zoom in on a sheet to judge whether the smallest text is still readable at that size.
  5. Print the PDF at 100% or 'fit to page' on A4 paper; for Letter paper, choose fit to page so nothing is clipped.

When to use this tool

  • Printing lecture slides as handouts, two or four to a sheet, with room to write in the margins.
  • Proofreading a long draft on paper while using half or a quarter of the sheets.
  • Making compact reference copies of manuals, forms or checklists to keep in a folder.
  • Showing two consecutive pages side by side, like a spread, for a quick visual check of a layout.
  • Reducing printing costs for meeting packs where the content is mainly large headings and diagrams.
  • Turning a stack of small receipts or tickets saved one per page into a sheet you can print and file.

What to know before you start

  • Only 2-up and 4-up are offered. There is no 6-up, 8-up or custom grid.
  • The output sheet is always landscape A4, whatever the size of your original pages. If you print on US Letter, select fit to page in the print dialog.
  • Portrait pages in 2-up mode sit side by side and fill most of the sheet height. Portrait pages in 4-up mode become quite small: an A4 page ends up at roughly a third of its original width and height.
  • Pages keep their reading order: in 4-up, page 1 is top left, 2 top right, 3 bottom left and 4 bottom right. If the page count is not a multiple of 2 or 4, the last sheet has empty cells. Blank cells stay white; nothing is repeated to fill them.
  • Links, form fields, comments and bookmarks are not carried over. The page content is copied as a drawing; interactive parts of the original are left behind.
  • Page rotation set in the original file may not be honoured by the embedding, so a page that only displays sideways because of a rotation flag can come out sideways. Fix it with Rotate PDF and save first if that happens.

Tips for the best results

  • For slides, 4-up usually works because slides are landscape and use large type. For portrait text documents, 2-up is the better trade-off between paper saved and readability.
  • Crop wide white margins first with Crop PDF. The content then fills more of each cell, and the text prints larger.
  • Remove blank or unnecessary pages before you make the N-up file, otherwise they still take up a cell.
  • If you want handouts in a particular order, reorder the pages with Organize Pages first; N-up places pages strictly in sequence.
  • Print a single sheet as a test before sending a long job to the printer.

A worked example: workshop handouts for an NGO training day

A small NGO runs a one-day training session for 25 volunteers and wants to give everyone the 48-slide deck on paper. One slide per page means 48 sheets per person, or 1,200 sheets in total.

The coordinator exports the deck to PDF and runs it through N-up with 4-up selected. The result has 12 landscape A4 sheets per person. Because the slides use large headings and simple diagrams, they remain readable at quarter size. Printed double-sided, that is 6 sheets of paper per volunteer, 150 in total.

Two slides contained dense tables that were too small at 4-up. The coordinator extracted those two pages and printed them separately at full size, rather than switching the whole deck to 2-up and doubling the paper. She then merged the N-up handout and the two full-size pages so each volunteer received one stapled set, and kept the original deck for projecting on the day.

Troubleshooting

Text is too small to read
Switch from 4-up to 2-up, crop the white margins of the original first, or print the dense pages separately at full size.
The printout is cut off at the edges
Your printer paper probably is not A4, or the printer cannot print to the edge. Choose fit to page or shrink oversized pages in the print dialog.
Some pages appear sideways
The original pages may rely on a rotation setting. Rotate the source pages so they are upright, save, and run N-up again.
Links in the original no longer work
That is expected: N-up copies the page content only. Keep the original file for on-screen reading.
The last sheet is half empty
The page count is not a multiple of the pages per sheet. Leave it, or remove or add pages before creating the N-up file.
The tool fails on a file
Damaged or unusual PDFs can fail to embed. Run the file through Repair PDF and try again with the repaired copy.

Frequently asked questions

Is the text still sharp after N-up?

Yes. Each page is embedded as vector content, not as an image, so text and line art print crisply even when scaled down.

Can I choose the output paper size?

No. The output is always landscape A4. Use your print dialog's fit-to-page option for other paper sizes.

Can I put 6 or 9 pages on a sheet?

Not at the moment. The tool offers 2-up and 4-up only. For more pages per sheet, your printer driver's multiple-pages option may help.

Does it add borders between pages?

It does not draw lines. Each page is scaled to 95% of its cell, which leaves a small white gap between pages.

What happens to pages of different sizes?

Each page is scaled separately to fit its own cell, keeping its proportions, so a mix of sizes will appear at different scales on the sheet.

Do I need an account?

No. The tool works directly in your browser with no sign-up.
