Spreadsheets are working documents — cells shift, formulas recalculate, and one accidental keystroke can quietly change a total. That is fine while you are building the numbers, and risky the moment you share them. PDFora's Excel to PDF converter turns your XLS or XLSX file into a clean, fixed PDF: a snapshot of your data that nobody can nudge out of shape.

The layout you see in Excel is the layout everyone gets. Column widths hold, headers repeat where you set them, and charts print exactly as designed. Conversion happens on PDFora's secure cloud engine — your spreadsheet is uploaded over an encrypted connection, rendered to PDF, and automatically deleted once processing ends. While the rest of PDFora's toolkit runs inside your browser, spreadsheet rendering needs a server, so this tool uses that encrypted pipeline.

As with everything on the site, it is free. No signup, no watermark across your figures, no page limits. Upload the workbook, download the PDF, and send your numbers out into the world in a format that behaves.

How to convert Excel to PDF online

  1. Tidy your spreadsheet in Excel — set the print area and page orientation if the sheet is wide.
  2. Open the Excel to PDF tool on getpdfora.com.
  3. Upload your XLS or XLSX file by dragging it onto the page or browsing for it.
  4. The secure engine renders your sheets to PDF, preserving column widths, cell formatting, and charts.
  5. Download the finished PDF to your device.
  6. Open it and check how the pages break — wide sheets sometimes read better in landscape, which takes one setting change and a reconvert.

When to use this tool

  • Send an invoice built in Excel as a PDF, so the client sees a tidy bill instead of a grid of editable cells.
  • Share a project budget with stakeholders who need to read the numbers, not accidentally rewrite them.
  • Submit expense reports or timesheets in a fixed format that approvers can file straight into their records.
  • Publish a price list or product catalog maintained in a spreadsheet as a clean PDF for customers.
  • Archive month-end financial statements as PDFs, freezing the figures exactly as they were reported.
  • Print a schedule, roster, or seating chart without wrestling with Excel's print preview on someone else's computer.

Tips for the best results

  • Set the print area in Excel before converting — it tells the renderer exactly which cells belong in the PDF and keeps stray columns out.
  • Wide spreadsheets almost always look better in landscape orientation; change it under Page Layout before you upload.
  • Use Excel's 'Fit Sheet on One Page' scaling for small tables so the PDF is not a big page with a tiny table in the corner.
  • Repeat header rows via Page Layout > Print Titles so multi-page tables stay readable all the way through.
  • Remember the PDF captures values, not formulas — keep the XLSX as your working master and treat the PDF as the published snapshot.

Frequently asked questions

Will my formatting and charts survive the conversion?

Yes. Cell colors, borders, fonts, merged cells, and embedded charts are rendered into the PDF just as they appear in Excel. Very wide sheets are the main thing to watch, since they may split across pages.

How do I stop my spreadsheet from spilling across many PDF pages?

Control it in Excel before converting: set a print area, switch to landscape, or use the scaling options to fit columns to one page width. The PDF follows whatever page setup the workbook carries.

Does the tool handle both XLS and XLSX?

Yes, both the modern XLSX format and legacy XLS files from older Excel versions convert fine. Files created in Google Sheets or LibreOffice and saved as XLSX work too.

Is my financial data safe here?

The conversion uses PDFora's secure cloud engine: your workbook travels over an encrypted connection, is converted, and is automatically deleted after processing. No copies are kept and nothing is shared.

Are formulas visible in the PDF?

No — the PDF shows only the calculated results, exactly as the cells display them. That is usually an advantage when sharing, since your working logic stays private inside the original file.

Is there any cost, signup, or watermark?

None of the three. The Excel to PDF converter is free, works without an account, and produces clean output with no branding added to your pages.
