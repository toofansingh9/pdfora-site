Spreadsheets were never designed to be pages. A sheet can be 40 columns wide and 3,000 rows long, and Excel only decides where it splits into pages at the moment you print. Ask for a PDF without preparing for that and you get the classic result: columns K to P stranded on page 7, headers on page one only, and a tiny chart floating on a page of its own. Getting a good PDF out of a workbook is mostly about telling the workbook how it should be printed.

This converter takes one .xls or .xlsx file, uploads it over an encrypted connection to the CloudConvert service via PDFora's relay, and returns a PDF named after the workbook. A spreadsheet engine on the server recalculates the layout there; nothing is converted inside your browser. The files are deleted from the service once the job is done. There are no options on the page itself; the result follows the page setup saved inside the workbook.

That last point is the key to good output. Print areas, orientation, scaling such as Fit All Columns on One Page, repeated title rows, headers, footers and manual page breaks are all stored in the file, and they are what decides how your PDF looks. A few minutes in Excel's Page Layout tab before converting saves far more time than converting several times and hoping.

How to convert Excel to PDF

  1. In Excel, open Page Layout, set the print area, orientation and scaling for each sheet you want in the PDF, then save and close the workbook.
  2. Open Excel to PDF and choose the .xlsx or .xls file.
  3. Click Convert. The file is uploaded, converted on the server and the status line shows progress.
  4. Download the PDF. It carries the workbook's name with a .pdf extension.
  5. Page through it and check that no columns were split off and that figures, especially formula results, match the workbook.

When to use this tool

  • Monthly rent statements a landlord keeps in a spreadsheet, sent to tenants as PDFs they cannot alter.
  • Price lists and quotations for customers who do not have Excel or should not see the formulas behind the prices.
  • Financial summaries and budgets attached to board papers or grant applications that require PDF.
  • Timetables, rotas and seating plans printed and pinned on a noticeboard.
  • Archiving year-end figures in a format that will still open the same way in ten years.
  • Sharing a report from a phone or tablet where Excel's own export is missing or awkward.

What to know before you start

  • Without a print area, the converter generally prints the whole used range of each sheet, including stray cells far from your table that once held a value. Set a print area to control exactly what appears.
  • Wide sheets are split across pages column by column unless the workbook is scaled. Fit All Columns on One Page (in Page Setup > Scaling) is usually the right choice for tables up to about 15 columns; beyond that, use landscape as well, or accept that the text will get small.
  • Several sheets normally appear one after another in the PDF. Hidden sheets, rows and columns are usually left out, so hiding is a simple way to keep a working sheet out of the output.
  • Formulas are recalculated by the server's engine. Standard functions give the same results, but workbooks that rely on macros, add-ins or external links may show stale values or errors. Paste-special the values into a copy if you need certainty.
  • Charts and conditional formatting normally come through, though fonts and fine details can differ slightly from Excel's own rendering.
  • Only one workbook is converted per run, and the tool waits about three minutes for the server. Very large workbooks with tens of thousands of rows can exceed that.

Tips for the best results

  • Use Page Layout > Print Titles to repeat the header row at the top of every page. A 12-page table without headers is nearly unreadable.
  • Turn on View > Page Break Preview in Excel and drag the blue lines; what you see there is close to how the PDF will be paginated.
  • Put the sheets you want in the PDF first and hide or delete scratch sheets before converting.
  • Add a footer with page numbers (Page &[Page] of &[Pages]) so printed copies can be put back in order.
  • If only one sheet matters, copy it into a new workbook and convert that; it is quicker than adjusting every sheet.

A worked example: a landlord's annual statement

A landlord with six flats keeps rents, repairs and service charges in one workbook: a Summary sheet, a sheet per flat, and a scratch sheet with notes. He wants one tenant's annual statement as a PDF. Converted as it stands, the result is 23 pages long. It includes every flat, the notes, and a 14-column ledger split so that the Balance column sits alone on its own page.

He copies the tenant's sheet into a new workbook, so the other tenants' details are not in the file at all. On that sheet he sets the print area to A1:N60, switches to landscape, chooses Fit All Columns on One Page, sets row 3 (the headings) as a print title, and adds a footer with the date and page numbers. He saves the file and converts it again.

The new PDF is two landscape pages with headings on both, the balance visible next to each month, and nothing about the other flats. Copying the sheet first was the important step: hiding sheets would have kept other tenants' data inside the workbook, even though it would not appear in the PDF.

Troubleshooting

Columns are cut off onto separate pages
The sheet is wider than the page. In Excel, set Page Setup > Scaling to Fit All Columns on One Page and switch to landscape, save, and convert again.
The PDF has dozens of nearly blank pages
Stray formatting or values far down or to the right extend the used range. Set a print area, or delete the unused rows and columns below and beside your data and save.
Some cells show #NAME? or #REF! in the PDF
The workbook uses a function, add-in, macro or external link the server cannot evaluate. Copy the sheet, paste values only, and convert the copy.
Sheets I did not want are in the PDF
All visible sheets are usually converted. Hide or delete the sheets you do not need, or copy the relevant sheet into a new workbook.
The conversion failed straight away
The workbook may be password-protected to open, damaged, or in a format other than .xls/.xlsx (for example .xlsm or .csv). Remove the password, save a fresh .xlsx copy, and try again.
It timed out
Very large workbooks can take longer than the tool waits. Convert only the sheets you need, or split the data into smaller workbooks.

Frequently asked questions

Is my spreadsheet uploaded to a server?

Yes. The workbook is sent encrypted to the conversion service, converted there and deleted afterwards. Use Excel's own Save as PDF for data that must not leave your computer.

Can I convert just one sheet?

There is no sheet picker. Hide the other sheets, or copy the one you want into a new workbook, and convert that file.

Will formulas still work in the PDF?

No. A PDF shows the calculated values only; the formulas themselves are not included. That is often the point when sharing prices or totals.

Can I convert CSV or Google Sheets files?

The tool accepts .xls and .xlsx. Open a CSV in a spreadsheet program, or download a Google Sheet as .xlsx, and convert that.

How do I keep the header row on every page?

Set it in Excel under Page Layout > Print Titles > Rows to repeat at top, save, and convert. The setting is stored in the workbook and followed by the converter.

Why are charts or fonts slightly different from Excel?

The server uses its own spreadsheet engine and fonts, so fine rendering details can differ. Data and layout should match; use common fonts for the closest result.
