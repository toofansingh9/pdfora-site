A scanned PDF looks like a document but behaves like a photo album: you cannot search it, copy a paragraph from it or paste a figure into a spreadsheet, because each page is only a picture of text. Optical character recognition (OCR) looks at those pictures and works out which letters they show. PDFora's OCR tool does that for every page of a PDF and gives you the recognised text as a .txt file you can search, edit and reuse.

Be clear on what comes out. The current tool produces plain text, not a searchable PDF. Each page is rendered at about 144 pixels per inch, passed to Tesseract.js (an open-source OCR engine compiled to run in the browser), and the text it reads is written to the file page after page, with a blank line between pages. There is no layout: columns, tables and fonts are not reconstructed. A searchable-PDF output, where invisible text sits behind the page image, is planned but not available yet. Expect the output to need light cleaning: hyphenated line breaks stay hyphenated, and headers or page numbers are read as ordinary lines of text.

Recognition runs on your own device. The first run downloads the Tesseract engine and the language data from a public CDN, which can take a while on a slow connection; your PDF itself is not part of that download and is not sent anywhere.

How to extract text from a scanned PDF

  1. Open the OCR tool and add one PDF. Scans, phone photos saved as PDF and image-only exports all work.
  2. Choose the language of the document: English, Hindi, Spanish or French. English is selected by default.
  3. Click Run OCR. The status line shows which page is being read; expect several seconds per page, longer on a phone.
  4. Download the result, named after the original with -ocr.txt added, for example lease-ocr.txt.
  5. Open the text file and proofread names, numbers and amounts against the scan before you rely on them.

When to use this tool

  • Copying clauses from a scanned contract into a reply email instead of retyping them.
  • Turning printed meeting minutes or NGO field reports into editable text for a newsletter or database.
  • Pulling quotes from photocopied book chapters or journal articles for study notes, with page breaks preserved.
  • Extracting addresses and reference numbers from a stack of scanned letters to search through them later.
  • Reading Hindi or Spanish documents into text that can be pasted into a translation tool.
  • Checking whether a PDF already has a text layer: if PDF to Text returns nothing but OCR returns text, the file is a pure scan.

What to know before you start

  • The output is a plain .txt file. It holds the words in reading order as the engine sees them, with no formatting, images or page layout.
  • Four languages are offered: English, Hindi, Spanish and French. A document in another language will be read with the wrong character models and give poor results.
  • Accuracy depends mostly on the scan. Clean, straight, high-contrast printed pages usually come out well; handwriting, faint photocopies, stamps over text and skewed phone photos come out with many errors.
  • Tables and multi-column layouts are read as lines of text. Columns may be interleaved, and table cells lose their grid.
  • Pages are rendered at about 144 pixels per inch before recognition. Very small print, such as footnotes under 8 pt, may fall below what the engine can read reliably at that resolution.
  • Every page is OCR'd, even pages that already contain real text. For a mixed document, PDF to Text is faster and exact for the digital pages.

Tips for the best results

  • Scan at 300 dpi in black and white or grayscale if you control the scanner; it helps recognition more than any setting in the tool.
  • Rotate sideways pages upright with Rotate PDF first. The engine expects text to run left to right on an upright page.
  • For a long document, split it and OCR only the pages you need. Recognition time grows page by page.
  • Proofread numbers carefully. Confusions such as 0 and O, 1 and l, or 5 and S are the most common OCR errors and the most costly in invoices and IDs.
  • Keep the original scan alongside the text file; the text is a working copy, the scan is the record.

A worked example: a student and a photocopied reader

A history student has a 22-page course reader that the department scanned from photocopies. She needs about a dozen quotations for an essay and wants to search the reader for every mention of a particular treaty rather than skim it again.

She runs the PDF through OCR with English selected. It takes a few minutes on her laptop. The resulting text file is searchable at once, and the blank line between pages lets her work out which page each quote came from for her citations. Two pages photocopied at a slant come out with garbled lines; she rotates and re-scans those two in the library, OCRs them separately and pastes the clean text in.

She checks every quotation she uses against the scan, since a misread word in a quotation is worse than none, and fixes one date where the engine read 1648 as 1043.

The text file is also what she uses to build her bibliography: the publication details on each chapter's title page come through cleanly, because title pages are usually printed in large, well-spaced type. Small footnotes are where most of the errors cluster, so those she reads straight from the scan instead of trusting the recognised version.

Troubleshooting

The text file is empty or nearly empty
The pages may be very low contrast, very small or blank. Open the PDF and zoom in: if you struggle to read it, the engine will too. Re-scan with higher contrast if possible.
The output is full of nonsense characters
Check the language setting matches the document. Also check the pages are upright; sideways or upside-down pages produce garbage. Rotate them first.
The tool hangs at the start
The first run downloads the OCR engine and language data. On a slow or filtered network, or with a script blocker, this can stall. Allow cdn.jsdelivr.net and try again.
Columns are mixed together
Multi-column pages are read as lines across the page in some cases. Crop each column into its own page with Crop PDF, or copy the passages you need and tidy them by hand.
It is very slow on my phone
OCR is demanding. Each page is recognised on your device's processor. Use a desktop for long documents, or OCR only the pages you need after splitting.
I wanted a searchable PDF, not text
Searchable-PDF output is not available yet. For now, use the text file for searching and copying, and keep the scan as the visual record.

Frequently asked questions

Does this tool make my PDF searchable?

Not yet. It outputs recognised text as a .txt file. Searchable-PDF output is planned.

Which languages are supported?

English, Hindi, Spanish and French. Choose the one that matches the document before running OCR.

Can it read handwriting?

Poorly. The engine is trained on printed text; neat block capitals sometimes work, cursive handwriting usually does not.

Is my document uploaded for recognition?

No. The engine is downloaded to your browser and recognition runs there; only the engine and language files are fetched from the CDN.

Why does each page take several seconds?

Each page is rendered as an image and analysed for character shapes on your own processor, which is far heavier work than reading existing text.

Does it keep tables and formatting?

No. The output is plain text in reading order. Table structure, fonts and images are not reproduced.
