Sometimes the PDF is just a container and what you actually want is inside it: the product photos in a supplier catalogue, the pictures in a newsletter, the logo on a letterhead. Taking screenshots gives you images at whatever size your screen happens to show, cropped by hand. Extracting them gets the pictures as they are stored in the file, at their own resolution, without the surrounding page.

Here is how this tool finds them. For each page, PDF.js lists the drawing operations and the tool picks out the ones that paint an image object. Each such image is decoded and written to a PNG file at its native pixel width and height. A photo placed in the document at 2000 x 1500 pixels comes out at 2000 x 1500, even if it was shown as a small thumbnail on the page. The files are named after the PDF plus a running number, for example catalogue-img-001.png, and packed into catalogue-images.zip.

It is not a page converter. Text, vector drawings, charts built from lines and shapes, and backgrounds made of colour fills are not images in the PDF sense, so they are not extracted. A logo that was saved as vector artwork will not appear, and a scanned document will usually produce one large image per page, because each scanned page is stored as a single picture.

How to extract images from a PDF

  1. Open Extract Images and add one PDF.
  2. Click Extract images. The tool walks through every page looking for image objects; the progress bar follows the pages.
  3. When it finishes, the result lists how many files were found. Download them all in one ZIP or pick individual files from the list (up to 50 buttons are shown).
  4. Open the PNGs and delete the ones you do not need; decorative elements such as bullets or background textures often appear too.
  5. If you need the picture as it appears on the page, cropped and with overlaid text, use PDF to PNG instead and crop the page image.

When to use this tool

  • A small online shop recovering product photos from a supplier's PDF catalogue when the original image files were never sent.
  • A non-profit reusing photos from last year's annual report in a new presentation, after the designer who made it has moved on.
  • Pulling the figures out of a research paper to look at them at full resolution.
  • Saving a logo or signature image that was embedded as a picture in a letter or certificate.
  • Getting the individual page scans out of a scanned PDF as image files for an image editor.
  • Checking what resolution the photos in a print file really have before sending it to a printer.

What to know before you start

  • Only image objects that pages draw are found. Vector logos, charts drawn with lines, and text are not images and will not be extracted.
  • Every output file is PNG, whatever the original format. A JPEG photo inside the PDF is decoded and saved losslessly as PNG, which is often larger than the original JPEG data.
  • Images are saved at their stored pixel size, not at the size they appear on the page. Crops applied by the page layout are not applied, so you may see more of the photo than the page showed.
  • An image used on several pages is saved once per use, so a logo on 20 pages yields 20 identical files.
  • Images drawn inline in the page content (rather than as separate objects), and some uncommon colour formats, are skipped. Each image also has a 4-second limit to decode; images that take longer are skipped rather than freezing the tool.
  • Soft masks and clipping may not be applied the way the page shows them, so a cut-out image can arrive with its full rectangular background. Images with transparency are written as PNG with their colours decoded by PDF.js; colour spaces such as CMYK are converted to RGB on the way, so colours can shift slightly compared with a print proof.

Tips for the best results

  • Run it on the original, uncompressed PDF. A file that has already been through compression contains downscaled images, and that is what you would get back.
  • Sort the extracted files by size to find the real photos quickly; tiny files are usually icons, bullets or textures.
  • If you need JPGs for the web, convert the PNGs in an image editor or batch tool after extracting, choosing your own compression.
  • For vector logos, ask for the original artwork; extraction cannot recover vector shapes as an image.
  • Check usage rights before reusing photos from someone else's document. Extracting an image does not change who owns it. Extraction reads every page in order, so a long catalogue full of photos takes a while and holds the decoded images in memory until you download them.

A worked example: a shop owner and a supplier catalogue

A shop owner selling kitchenware wants to list 30 new products online. The supplier sent a 24-page PDF catalogue but not the photos themselves, and repeated requests have gone unanswered.

Extract Images returns 41 PNG files. Thirty are the product shots, each 1200 to 1600 pixels wide, much larger than they appeared in the catalogue's grid; the rest are the supplier's logo repeated on several pages and a few background patterns. Sorting by file size separates them in a minute. Because the photos are lossless PNG, they are larger than needed for a shop page, so she exports them as JPG at a quality she chooses before uploading.

Two products are missing. On those pages the 'photos' turn out to be illustrations drawn as vector graphics, so there was no image object to extract. For those, she converts the pages with PDF to PNG at High and crops the products from the page images.

Troubleshooting

The tool says no extractable images were found
The PDF draws everything as text and vector shapes, or uses inline images the tool does not read. Convert the pages with PDF to PNG or PDF to JPG and crop what you need.
I got dozens of copies of the same logo
The logo is drawn on every page and each use is saved. Delete the duplicates, or sort by file name and keep one.
The extracted photo shows parts that were cropped on the page
The page layout crops or masks the image when displaying it, but the full stored image is extracted. Crop it again in an image editor.
The images are smaller than I hoped
The PDF only contains them at that resolution, often because it was compressed for email. Ask for the original file or the original images.
The PNG files are much bigger than the PDF
JPEG photos in the PDF are decoded and saved as lossless PNG. Re-save them as JPG if size matters.
The file will not load
It may be protected with a password or damaged. Remove the password with Unlock PDF or in the original application, or run Repair PDF first.

Frequently asked questions

What format are the extracted images?

All images are saved as PNG, regardless of how they were stored in the PDF.

Are the images extracted at full resolution?

Yes, at the pixel size they are stored in the PDF, which can be larger than they appear on the page.

Why is my logo not among the extracted files?

It is most likely vector artwork, not an embedded picture. Use PDF to PNG to capture it as part of a page image.

Does it extract images from scanned PDFs?

Yes. A scanned page is usually stored as one picture, so you get one image per page.

Why are some images missing?

Inline images, some unusual colour formats and images that take more than 4 seconds to decode are skipped.

Are the images uploaded anywhere?

No. Decoding and PNG creation happen in your browser tab with PDF.js and the files go straight into your download.
