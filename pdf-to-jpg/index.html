PDF to JPG

A PDF is awkward in places that expect a picture. Chat apps show it as a file icon, slide decks will not accept it as an image, and many web forms and social platforms only take JPG or PNG. Converting the pages to JPG solves that: each page becomes an ordinary photo-style image that any phone, browser or editor can open without a PDF reader.

What the tool does is simple to describe and worth understanding. PDF.js draws every page onto a canvas exactly as a viewer would display it, and that canvas is then saved as a JPEG at quality 0.92. The quality menu controls the pixel size, not the compression: Screen renders at 1.5 times the page's point size (about 108 dpi), Standard at 2 times (about 144 dpi) and High at 3 times (about 216 dpi). An A4 page therefore comes out roughly 893 x 1263 pixels on Screen, 1191 x 1684 on Standard and 1786 x 2526 on High.

Because the result is a flat picture, everything that made the page a PDF is gone: text can no longer be selected or searched, links do not click, and form fields are just drawings of boxes. JPEG compression also adds faint smudging around sharp edges such as small black text on white. For photos and scans that is invisible; for line drawings, charts and screenshots of code, the PNG converter is usually the better choice.

How to convert a PDF to JPG images

  1. Open PDF to JPG and add one PDF. The tool handles a single file per run; convert other files one after another.
  2. Choose the Quality setting: Screen for chat and email previews, Standard (the default) for most uses, High when the image will be printed or zoomed into.
  3. Click Convert. Pages are rendered one at a time and the progress bar moves with each page, so long documents take a little while.
  4. Download everything with Download all (.zip), or click an individual file button to save just one page. The list shows buttons for up to the first 50 pages.
  5. Open a couple of the images at 100% zoom and check that small print is still readable before you post or send them.

When to use this tool

  • Posting a one-page event flyer, menu or notice on social media or in a WhatsApp group, where a picture displays inline but a PDF does not.
  • Dropping a page from a report into a PowerPoint or Google Slides deck as an image, so the layout cannot shift on someone else's computer.
  • Uploading a document to a website form that only accepts JPG, such as some exam, job or government portals that ask for a scanned certificate as an image.
  • Making thumbnails or preview images of a brochure or ebook for a product page or blog post.
  • Sending a signed page to someone who reads everything on a phone and struggles with PDF attachments.
  • Feeding pages into a photo editor to crop, annotate or combine them with other graphics.

What to know before you start

  • Every page is converted. There is no page-range option, so if you only need pages 4 to 6, run Extract Pages first and convert the smaller file.
  • Output files are named after the PDF with a three-digit page number: report.pdf gives report-001.jpg, report-002.jpg and so on, packed in report-jpg.zip. The padding keeps them in order when sorted by name.
  • JPEG has no transparency. Pages are rendered on a white background, so a PDF with a transparent or empty background produces white areas, not see-through ones.
  • Pages with a non-standard size keep their proportions. A landscape spreadsheet page becomes a wide image, a receipt-shaped page becomes a tall thin one.
  • The dpi figures assume the page size stored in the PDF. A scan that was saved with an unusual page size will render at a pixel size that follows that page size, not the original scan resolution.
  • Rendering happens in the browser, so very long documents at High quality use a lot of memory. A 200-page file at 216 dpi is a lot of large images held at once; phones may slow down or reload the tab.

Tips for the best results

  • Use Standard for anything that will be viewed on a screen. High has four times the pixels of Screen and mostly produces bigger files nobody zooms into.
  • If the page is mostly text or diagrams with thin lines, try PDF to PNG as well and compare; PNG keeps edges crisp where JPG softens them.
  • When a portal sets a maximum file size, Screen quality is the quickest way to get under it without touching an image editor.
  • Rename the images after downloading if they will be uploaded separately; report-003.jpg means little to the person receiving it.
  • Keep the original PDF. Converting back from JPG to PDF gives you an image-only document without selectable text.

A worked example: a café menu for Instagram and a printed board

A small café owner has a two-page menu designed in a word processor and exported as A4 PDF. She wants page 1 as an Instagram post and both pages on a framed board by the counter. Posting the PDF directly is impossible, and screenshots from her phone come out blurry with the status bar in the corner.

For Instagram she runs PDF to JPG on Standard. Page 1 comes out at about 1191 x 1684 pixels, which is more than the app needs and comfortably sharp on any phone. For the board she converts again on High, which gives about 1786 x 2526 pixels per page, roughly 216 dots per inch when printed at A4 — enough for text that is read from a metre away. The two runs produce menu-001.jpg and menu-002.jpg each time, so she keeps them in separate folders named web and print.

The one thing she checks is the price list in small type. At Screen quality it was readable but soft; at Standard it was clean. That is a typical result: the smaller your smallest font, the higher the setting you need.

Troubleshooting

The text in the images looks fuzzy
Increase the Quality setting to High. If it is still soft, the source page may itself be a low-resolution scan; rendering cannot add detail that is not in the PDF. For sharp text and line art, use PDF to PNG.
The PDF will not load
The file may be password-protected or damaged. Remove the password in the application that created it or with Unlock PDF if you know the password, or run it through Repair PDF, then try again.
I only got some pages as separate download buttons
The result list shows individual buttons for the first 50 pages only. The Download all (.zip) button always contains every page.
The images are too large to upload
Convert again with the Screen setting, or reduce the images in an image editor. JPEG quality is fixed at 0.92, so the resolution setting is the lever here.
Some fonts or symbols look different from my PDF viewer
PDF.js substitutes a fallback font when a font is not embedded in the PDF. Re-export the PDF with fonts embedded, then convert it again.
The browser became slow or the tab reloaded
High quality on a long document uses a lot of memory. Split the PDF into smaller parts or use Standard, and close other heavy tabs.

Frequently asked questions

What resolution are the JPG files?

Screen is 1.5x, Standard 2x and High 3x the PDF page size in points, which works out at about 108, 144 and 216 dpi. An A4 page at Standard is roughly 1191 x 1684 pixels.

Can I choose the JPEG compression level?

No. Compression is fixed at quality 0.92, which keeps visible artefacts low. File size is controlled through the resolution preset instead.

Will the text in the images be searchable?

No. Each page is a flat picture. If you need text later, keep the PDF, or run the images through OCR.

Can I convert only certain pages?

Not in this tool; it converts all pages. Use Extract Pages to make a PDF containing just the pages you need, then convert that.

Why is there a white background where my PDF was transparent?

JPEG cannot store transparency, so the page is drawn over white. If you need transparency, JPG is the wrong format for that page.

Is the PDF uploaded when I convert it?

No. PDF.js renders the pages inside your browser tab and the JPGs are created there, so the document is not sent to a server for this conversion.
