Split by Size

Break a PDF into parts that each stay under a size you set in megabytes, cutting only between whole pages.

Attachment and upload limits are measured in megabytes, not pages, which is why splitting by page count so often fails: the half with the scanned photos is still too big. Split by Size works the other way round. You type the largest file you are allowed to send, for example 5 MB (the default), and the tool fills each output with as many consecutive pages as fit under that figure.

Here is what it does internally. Starting with an empty PDF, it copies in one page at a time and saves the document after each page to measure its real size. As soon as adding a page pushes the file over your limit, that page is taken back out, the part is finished, and the page starts the next part. Because the size is measured on the actual saved file, including fonts and images, the parts come out under the limit rather than roughly near it. This page-by-page measuring is also why the parts are not equal in size: a part ends exactly when the next page would no longer fit.

There is one exception you must know about: a page is never cut in half. If a single page is itself larger than your limit, for instance a 7 MB high-resolution scan with a 5 MB limit, that page gets a part of its own and that part will exceed the limit. Outputs are numbered after the original, so scan.pdf yields scan-1.pdf, scan-2.pdf and so on, inside scan-bysize.zip.

How to split a PDF by file size

  1. Open Split by Size and add the PDF you need to send.
  2. Enter the maximum size per file in MB. Decimals work (for example 9.5), and the smallest value accepted is 0.1.
  3. Click Split by size. The progress bar advances page by page because every page is measured.
  4. Download the zip or the individual parts.
  5. Check the sizes of the parts in your file manager before uploading; any part above the limit is a single oversized page.

When to use this tool

  • Sending a large scanned contract by email when the mail server rejects attachments over a fixed size. Each part arrives as a complete PDF the recipient can open on its own.
  • Government and university portals that accept several uploads but cap each one.
  • Insurance claims with many photos, where the claim form allows multiple small attachments.
  • Archiving into systems that refuse files above a certain size.
  • Splitting a tender response for a buyer's procurement site with per-file limits.
  • Messaging apps with a per-file limit, when a long scanned document has to reach someone on a phone.

What to know before you start

  • The limit is computed as your number times 1,048,576 bytes, that is, binary megabytes. Some websites count 1 MB as 1,000,000 bytes, so setting your value a little below the stated limit (4.8 instead of 5) leaves a safety margin.
  • Pages stay in their original order and each part is a continuous run of pages. The tool does not reshuffle pages to pack the parts more tightly.
  • A single page larger than the limit cannot be made smaller by splitting. Compress the PDF first, or that page will produce an oversized part.
  • Fonts and images used on many pages are embedded in every part that uses them. A text document with heavy embedded fonts may therefore give more parts than a simple division of sizes suggests.
  • Because the document is re-saved after every page, the tool is slower than the other split tools on long files. A few hundred pages can take a noticeable while on a phone. Short documents finish almost instantly.
  • Bookmarks are not copied, and links that point to pages that ended up in another part no longer work.

Tips for the best results

  • Compress first, then split. Compression often reduces scanned PDFs dramatically, and you may end up needing fewer parts or none at all.
  • Leave a margin of 5 to 10 percent under the official limit; email systems also add encoding overhead to attachments, which makes a file larger in transit than on disk. Base64 encoding used for email attachments adds roughly a third to the size, so a mail server with a 10 MB limit may reject a file well below 10 MB on disk.
  • Tell the recipient how many parts to expect, for example 'parts 1 to 4 of 4', so they know when they have everything.
  • If the parts should end at logical sections, check where the cuts fell and adjust with Split PDF using custom ranges.
  • For a document that is only slightly over the limit, try Split in Half first: it is quicker and gives the recipient only two files.

Example: a freelancer's portfolio against a 10 MB email limit

A freelance interior designer has a 38-page portfolio PDF of 31 MB, mostly full-page photographs of finished rooms. A prospective client's company mail server bounces anything over 10 MB, and they have asked for the portfolio by email rather than a download link.

Setting the limit to 9.5 MB instead of 10 leaves room for the email encoding overhead. Split by Size returns four parts of roughly 9.3, 9.1, 8.9 and 3.7 MB; because pages are distributed in order, the number of pages in each part varies with how heavy the photos on those pages are. The designer sends them as four emails titled 'Portfolio 1/4' to '4/4'. No part was cut mid-page, and every part opened as a complete, normal PDF.

A better alternative existed: running Compress PDF on the Recommended level first brought the file to around 12 MB without visible loss on screen, after which a split at 9.5 MB gave just two parts. When the content is photographic, compression and splitting together almost always beat splitting alone.

Troubleshooting

One part is over my limit
That part contains a single page that on its own is larger than the limit. Compress the PDF or that page's image, then split again.
The upload still says the file is too large
The site may count megabytes in decimal or include encoding overhead. Lower the limit by 5 to 10 percent and split again.
I got far more parts than I expected
Embedded fonts and shared images are repeated in every part. Compress or flatten the document first, or raise the limit if the destination allows.
The progress bar is very slow
Every page triggers a full save to measure size. That is the cost of accurate parts. Close other tabs, use a desktop, or halve the document first and split each half.
The PDF will not open in the tool
Encrypted or damaged files fail to load. Remove the password in the original application or use Repair PDF, then retry.
I only got one part
The whole document is already under your limit, so no cut was needed. Check the original file size; if it really is larger, make sure you entered the limit in MB and not in KB.

Frequently asked questions

Does splitting by size reduce image quality?

No. Pages are copied unchanged; nothing is recompressed. To make files smaller rather than more numerous, use Compress PDF.

Can a page be split across two parts?

No. A page is the smallest unit. A single page larger than the limit ends up alone in an oversized part.

What is the default limit?

5 MB. You can enter any value from 0.1 MB upwards, including decimals.

Is 1 MB here 1,000 KB or 1,024 KB?

The tool uses 1,048,576 bytes per MB. If your target counts in decimal megabytes, set a slightly lower value.

Are pages reordered to fill the parts evenly?

No. Parts are consecutive page runs in the original order, so the parts have different page counts.

How are the parts named?

With the original name and a number, such as file-1.pdf and file-2.pdf, in a zip called file-bysize.zip.
