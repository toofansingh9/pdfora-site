A form arrives by email, you have no printer, and the sender wants it back signed today. Fill & Sign handles that case: you open the PDF, type your answers where the blanks are, add a signature you draw with a mouse, finger or stylus (or type your name in a script font), and download a new file ending in -signed.pdf.

It is important to understand what kind of signature this produces. It is a picture of your signature placed on the page, the digital version of signing a printout and scanning it back. It is not a cryptographic digital signature with a certificate, and the file will not show a 'signed by' panel in a PDF reader. For most everyday paperwork that is exactly what the other side expects; for documents where the recipient specifically asks for a certificate-based or qualified electronic signature, it is not enough.

Under the hood this is the same layer editor as Edit PDF. Pages are rendered with PDF.js, your answers and signature sit on an overlay, and on save each page's overlay is turned into one image and stamped over the original page with pdf-lib. The form's printed content underneath is not changed.

How to fill in and sign a PDF

  1. Open Fill & Sign and drop in the PDF. All pages are drawn on screen, one under the other.
  2. Choose Text, set the size so it fits the blanks (10 to 12 suits most forms), click at the start of a blank line and type. Repeat for each field; drag a box to nudge it into position.
  3. Choose Signature and click where you want to sign. In the window that opens, draw on the pad or switch to Type and enter your name, then press Insert.
  4. Drag the signature onto the line and use its corner handle to resize it. Add a date next to it with another Text box.
  5. Click Save PDF, open the -signed.pdf download and check every page before returning it.

When to use this tool

  • Tenancy paperwork: inventory sheets and renewal letters that need a tenant's signature and date on the last page.
  • School consent slips for trips, photography or medical information sent home as PDFs.
  • Freelance contracts and statements of work that a client sends for countersignature.
  • Bank or HR forms delivered as flat PDFs with lines to write on rather than real fillable fields.
  • Delivery notes and timesheets that need a quick signature before being emailed back.
  • Volunteer and membership applications for clubs and NGOs.

What to know before you start

  • This tool does not fill real form fields. If your PDF already has interactive boxes (you can click into them in a normal reader), type into those in your PDF reader instead or Fill & Sign will simply print text over the top of them.
  • The signature is an image, not a certificate-based digital signature. It proves nothing on its own about who placed it, and anyone with the file can see that it is a picture.
  • Drawn signatures come from a pad 420 × 170 pixels in dark navy ink. Typed signatures are rendered in a cursive system font such as Segoe Script or Brush Script, so they look different on Windows and Mac.
  • Signatures are inserted at up to 180 pixels wide and can be enlarged, but because they are bitmap images they get blurry if you stretch them well beyond that.
  • Everything you type is merged into an image when saving, so your answers are not searchable and cannot be copied out of the finished file.
  • Your signature is not stored anywhere. Close the tab and it is gone; next time you have to draw it again, which also means nobody else on the computer can reuse it.

Tips for the best results

  • Draw the signature slowly with a stylus or finger on a touch screen if you have one. A mouse-drawn signature works but tends to look shaky.
  • Pick the text size before clicking: each box keeps the size and colour that were selected when it was created.
  • Use blue or black ink colours for answers. Bright colours can disappear when the recipient prints in greyscale.
  • Line text up by placing it slightly above the printed line rather than on it; the saved text sits marginally lower than many people expect.
  • Keep an unsigned copy of the original PDF. If you make a mistake after saving, it is quicker to start again from the clean file than to cover up errors.

Example: a parent signing a school trip form

A parent receives a one-page PDF consent form for a museum visit. It has printed lines for the child's name, class, an emergency phone number and a medical note, then 'Signature of parent/guardian' and 'Date'. Clicking on the lines in a PDF reader does nothing, so these are not real fields; the school expects it printed and signed.

In Fill & Sign the parent picks Text at size 11, clicks just above each line and types the answers, writing 'None' in the medical box. Then Signature, draw on the pad using a phone's touch screen, Insert, and drag it onto the signature line, shrinking it slightly so it does not cover the printed caption. A final Text box gets the date. The file downloads as trip-consent-signed.pdf and goes back to the class teacher as an email attachment.

If the same school later sends a form with real fillable boxes, the better route is to type into those boxes in the phone's PDF viewer and use Fill & Sign only to add the signature image.

Troubleshooting

Insert does nothing in the signature window
The pad is empty or the Type box is blank. Draw at least one stroke or type a name, then press Insert. Pressing Insert with nothing on the pad simply closes the window.
My signature looks pixelated
It was stretched far beyond its inserted size. Delete it with the ×, click to insert a new one, and keep it close to the default size. Drawing larger and more slowly on the pad also helps.
My answers do not line up with the lines in the saved file
Text is redrawn at save time with a standard sans-serif font, which can shift it a few pixels. Place text a little above the line and check the download before sending.
The form already had boxes and now my text overlaps them
The PDF has interactive fields. Open the original in your PDF reader, type into its own fields and save, then open that copy here only to add the signature.
The recipient says the signature is 'not valid'
They need a certificate-based digital or qualified electronic signature, which this tool does not create. Ask them which signing service they accept, or print, sign by hand and scan.
Could not render this PDF
The file is password-protected or damaged. Remove the password in the application that produced it, or run it through Repair PDF, then try again.

Frequently asked questions

Is a signature added here legally binding?

That depends on the document and where you are. Many everyday agreements accept a signature image, much like a scanned signature; some documents require a certificate-based or qualified electronic signature. If in doubt, ask the recipient what they accept.

Can I type my signature instead of drawing it?

Yes. Switch to Type in the signature window and enter your name; it is rendered in a cursive font installed on your device.

Can I sign several places in the document?

Yes. Choose Signature and click each place; each click opens the pad and inserts a new copy. You can also add initials the same way.

Will the filled-in text be editable later?

No. Text and signatures are flattened into an image layer on each page when you save. To change an answer, start again from the original file.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. Drawing on a touch screen with a finger is often easier than with a mouse. Long documents may be slow on older phones because every page is rendered.

Why does my saved file have -signed in its name?

The tool names the output after your file with -signed.pdf added, so the unsigned original is never overwritten.
