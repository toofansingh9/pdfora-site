Resize PDF

Put every page of a PDF onto A4, US Letter, A5 or A3, scaled to fit and centred, so the whole document has one page size.

A PDF assembled from different sources rarely has one page size. A letter from the UK is A4, a scan from a US office is Letter, a phone photo converted to PDF can be any odd shape, and a court portal or print shop may reject the lot because it wants one standard size. Resize Pages fixes this by placing every page on a new sheet of the size you choose. The tool is also useful when nothing is wrong with the individual pages but the mix causes trouble later: printers that pause to ask for a different paper tray, booklet layouts that come out uneven, or a page-numbering step that places numbers at different positions on pages of different sizes.

There are four targets: A4 (595 by 842 points), US Letter (612 by 792), A5 (420 by 595) and A3 (842 by 1191), all in portrait. Each original page is scaled so that it fits entirely inside the new sheet while keeping its proportions, then centred. If the shapes differ, the leftover space becomes white bands at the top and bottom or at the sides. Nothing is stretched and nothing is cut off.

The page content is embedded into the new sheet as vector content with pdf-lib, so text and drawings stay sharp at any scale. Because a fresh document is built, the output is a clean copy of the visible pages; the download is named after the original plus the size, for example contract-A4.pdf. That also means any odd page boxes in the original, such as an old crop setting, are resolved: the new file shows exactly what was visible before, on a sheet of the chosen size.

How to change the page size of a PDF

  1. Open Resize Pages and select one PDF.
  2. Choose the target size: A4, US Letter, A5 or A3. A4 is selected by default.
  3. Click Resize pages. Each page is placed, scaled and centred on a new sheet.
  4. Download the file, which carries the size in its name, such as report-Letter.pdf.
  5. Check a landscape page and a page from a different source; they are the ones most likely to show white bands or a noticeable change in scale.

When to use this tool

  • Uploading documents to a government or court portal that accepts only A4 or only Letter.
  • Sending files between offices in Europe and North America, where A4 and Letter differ by a few millimetres and printers complain about the mismatch.
  • Turning a mixed set of scans and photos into a tidy document with one consistent page size.
  • Scaling A4 pages down to A5 to print a booklet or pocket guide.
  • Enlarging A4 drawings or plans onto A3 for easier reading on paper.
  • Preparing documents for a print shop that expects every page at one standard size.

What to know before you start

  • Pages are scaled to fit, never stretched or cropped. If the original's shape differs from the target, white bands appear on two sides.
  • All four targets are portrait. A landscape page is fitted inside a portrait sheet, so it ends up smaller with wide white bands above and below. Rotate such pages first if they should fill the sheet.
  • Small pages are scaled up and large pages are scaled down. Scaling a phone-sized page up to A3 will make vector text larger and sharp, but low-resolution scans will look softer.
  • Links, form fields, annotations and bookmarks are not copied into the resized file, because each page is redrawn as content on a new sheet. Keep the original if you need them.
  • Going from A4 to Letter or the reverse changes the scale only slightly, but the white bands are still there: A4 on Letter leaves small bands at the sides, Letter on A4 leaves bands at the top and bottom. In practice A4 and Letter content looks almost identical after conversion; the difference is in the bands, not in readability.
  • Resizing does not change the resolution of scanned images; it only changes how large they are drawn on the page.

Tips for the best results

  • If a few pages are landscape spreadsheets, rotate them to portrait with Rotate PDF before resizing, or accept that they will appear smaller.
  • Crop wide margins first if you are scaling down to A5; the content then fills more of the smaller page.
  • For an A5 booklet, check that the smallest text in the original will still be readable at about 70% of its size, which is what A4 to A5 means.
  • Do the resize as one of the last steps. Adding page numbers or headers after resizing places them correctly on the final page size.
  • When a recipient asks for 'A4', also ask whether they mean portrait only; this tool always produces portrait sheets.

A worked example: a landlord's tenancy file for a council portal

A landlord needs to upload a tenancy file to a local council portal that requires A4. The file combines the agreement (A4), a gas safety certificate scanned in the US Letter setting of an office copier, and three phone photos of meter readings saved as PDF at an odd tall shape.

He runs the combined file through Resize Pages with A4 selected. The agreement pages are unchanged in appearance. The Letter scan is shrunk slightly to fit the narrower A4 width and gets thin white bands at the top and bottom. The tall phone photos are fitted to the A4 height with white bands at the sides.

The portal accepts tenancy-A4.pdf on the first try. The only loss is that a link in the agreement to the deposit scheme's website no longer clicks, so he types the address into the covering message instead.

He also keeps the mixed-size original. If the council later asks for the gas certificate on its own, he can extract it from the original at full scan quality instead of from the resized copy, where it was shrunk to fit the A4 width.

Troubleshooting

There are white bands around my pages
The original page shape differs from the target. The tool fits without stretching or cropping, so the extra space stays white. Crop the original first if the bands come from existing margins.
Landscape pages became tiny
The target sheets are portrait. Rotate those pages to portrait first, or keep them in a separate file at their original size.
Links or form fields stopped working
Interactive elements are not carried into the new sheets. Fill and flatten forms before resizing, or keep the original for on-screen use.
Scanned pages look blurry after enlarging
Resizing scales the existing image; it cannot add detail. Rescan at a higher resolution if you need to enlarge scans to A3.
The output still prints at the wrong size
Check your print dialog. Options such as fit to printable area can shrink the page again. Print at actual size.
The tool fails to open the file
The PDF may be damaged. Run it through Repair PDF and resize the repaired copy.

Frequently asked questions

Will resizing distort my pages?

No. The scale is the same horizontally and vertically, so proportions are kept. Unused space becomes white.

Can I set a custom page size?

Not at present. The choices are A4, US Letter, A5 and A3.

Can I resize to landscape?

No. All target sizes are portrait. Landscape originals are fitted inside the portrait sheet.

Is text still searchable after resizing?

In most files, yes. Pages are embedded as vector content, so real text remains text. Scanned pages without OCR were never searchable in the first place.

Does the file get bigger?

Usually only slightly. The page content is reused, not re-rendered, so file size stays close to the original.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. It runs in a mobile browser; very long documents simply take longer.
