JPG / PNG to PDF

Put JPG and PNG images into one PDF, one image per page, either at the image's own size or centred on A4 or US Letter pages.

Phone photos of receipts, screenshots of an error, a scanned ID card saved as a JPG: sooner or later someone asks for them "as a single PDF". The awkward part is rarely the conversion itself. It is that the photos come out sideways, the pages are wildly different sizes, or the file is far too heavy to email. This page describes exactly what the Images to PDF tool does with each picture so you can predict the result before you click.

Each image becomes one page, in the order shown in the file list. The picture is embedded as it is: a JPG goes into the PDF as the same JPEG data, a PNG goes in as a PNG, and neither is resized or recompressed. That keeps quality identical to the source, but it also means the PDF weighs roughly what the images weigh together. Ten 3 MB phone photos give you a PDF of about 30 MB.

There is one setting, Page, with three choices. Fit to image size (the default) makes every page exactly as large as its picture. A4 page and US Letter put each image on a standard sheet, scaled down to fit inside a 20-point border (about 7 mm) and centred, with its proportions kept. The output downloads as images.pdf, and because the work is done by the pdf-lib library in your browser tab, the photos are not sent to a server first.

How to turn images into a PDF

  1. Open Images to PDF and add your JPG or PNG files. You can select several at once from the file picker or drop them onto the box.
  2. Drag the rows into reading order. The first row becomes page 1.
  3. Choose the Page setting: keep Fit to image size for on-screen viewing, or pick A4 or US Letter if the PDF will be printed or uploaded to a form that expects standard pages.
  4. Click Create PDF and wait for the progress bar; large photos take a moment each.
  5. Download images.pdf, rename it to something meaningful, and flick through it to check that no page is upside down or missing.

When to use this tool

  • Expense claims: photos of paper receipts collected over a trip, submitted to finance as one document.
  • Homework and assignments: photographed handwritten pages turned into a single file for a school portal that only accepts PDF.
  • Tenancy paperwork: pictures of a signed lease page, meter readings and the condition of each room, kept together with the inventory.
  • Bug reports and support tickets: a sequence of screenshots in order, so the reader can follow the steps without opening ten attachments.
  • Identity documents: front and back of an ID card or licence photographed separately and placed on A4 pages for an application.
  • Portfolios: a designer's PNG exports gathered into one PDF that a client can scroll through on any device.

What to know before you start

  • Only JPG and PNG are handled. The tool embeds any file that is not a PNG as a JPEG, so WebP, HEIC, GIF, BMP or TIFF files will fail. Convert those to JPG or PNG first; on an iPhone, setting the camera format to Most Compatible makes it save JPGs.
  • With Fit to image size, one image pixel becomes one PDF point (1/72 inch). A 4000 x 3000 phone photo therefore makes a page about 141 x 106 cm. It looks normal on screen because viewers zoom to fit, but printing it, or mixing it with a small screenshot, produces very uneven pages. Use A4 or Letter when that matters.
  • On A4 and Letter the image is only ever scaled to fit; it is not cropped and not rotated. A landscape photo placed on a portrait A4 page sits in a horizontal band with white space above and below.
  • The rotation flag that phones write into a photo's EXIF data is not applied. If your gallery app shows a photo upright only because of that flag, it may appear sideways in the PDF. Rotate the image in an editor and save it, or fix the page afterwards with Rotate PDF.
  • PNG transparency is kept as transparency, which most viewers show as white. Very large PNG screenshots can be several megabytes each, because PNG is lossless.
  • The pages are pictures, so their text cannot be selected or searched. Run the finished PDF through OCR if you need to copy text out of it.

Tips for the best results

  • Crop photos before you add them. A receipt photographed on a kitchen table carries the whole table into the PDF and into the file size.
  • For printing or official uploads, choose A4 (or US Letter in North America) so every page has the same size and the printer does not have to guess the scaling.
  • If the PDF is too large, resize the photos to around 2000 pixels on the long side before converting, or run the finished file through Compress PDF.
  • Name the images 01, 02, 03 before you add them; the list is then already close to the right order and easy to check.
  • Photograph documents in daylight, flat, from directly above. A clean image matters far more for legibility than any setting in the tool.

A worked example: an NGO field officer's expense claim

A field officer with a small NGO comes back from a four-day village visit with 14 paper receipts: bus tickets, a guesthouse bill, meals, printing. Finance wants one PDF attached to the claim form, printable on A4, and the shared mailbox rejects attachments over 10 MB. The receipts were photographed on a phone, each JPG about 2.5 MB at 4000 x 3000 pixels, so the raw total is around 35 MB.

First she crops each photo to the receipt edges in the phone's gallery editor and saves the copy; that removes the table background and brings most files under 1 MB. Two photos that showed up sideways in a test run are rotated in the editor and saved again, so the rotation is in the pixels and not just in the EXIF flag. She numbers the files in date order, adds all 14 to Images to PDF and picks A4 page.

The result is a 14-page PDF of about 9 MB, every page the same size, each receipt centred with a narrow white border, which prints cleanly at the office. To leave headroom under the limit she runs it through Compress PDF on the recommended level, and the amounts on even the faded thermal-paper bus tickets stay readable.

Troubleshooting

I get an error as soon as I click Create PDF
One of the files is probably not a real JPG or PNG. HEIC photos from iPhones, WebP images saved from websites and GIFs are not supported. Open the file in an image editor, export it as JPG or PNG, and add the new copy.
A photo is sideways or upside down in the PDF
Your phone stored the orientation as an EXIF flag instead of rotating the pixels, and the tool does not read that flag. Rotate the photo in an editor and save it, or rotate just that page afterwards with Rotate PDF.
The pages are all different sizes
That is what Fit to image size does: each page matches its image. Choose A4 page or US Letter to put every image on the same sheet size.
The PDF is much bigger than I expected
Images are embedded without recompression, so the PDF is about as large as the photos combined. Crop and downscale the images first, or compress the finished PDF.
The pages are in the wrong order
The order of rows in the list at the moment you click Create PDF is the page order. Drag the rows into place and create the PDF again.
I cannot search or copy the text in the PDF
Each page is a picture of the text, not text. Run the PDF through the OCR tool to extract the words.

Frequently asked questions

Which image formats can I convert?

JPG (JPEG) and PNG. Other formats such as HEIC, WebP, GIF or TIFF need to be converted to JPG or PNG first.

Does the tool reduce image quality?

No. JPEG and PNG data are embedded as they are, without resizing or recompressing, so each page shows the image at its original resolution.

What page size will my PDF have?

With the default setting each page has the pixel dimensions of its image, measured in points. Choose A4 (595 x 842 points) or US Letter (612 x 792 points) for standard pages with a small white border.

Can I mix portrait and landscape images?

Yes. With Fit to image size each page takes the orientation of its image. On A4 or Letter all pages are portrait and landscape images are scaled to fit the width.

Can I add more images to the PDF later?

Not by editing images.pdf in this tool. Create a second PDF from the new images and join the two with Merge PDF.

Why is the photo of my document blurry in the PDF?

The tool cannot add detail that was not in the photo. If the image is blurry or dark at full size in your gallery, it will look the same in the PDF; retake it in better light with the phone held steady.
