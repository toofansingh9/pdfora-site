A filled-out PDF form is still editable. Anyone who opens it can click into a field, change the amount, tick a different box and save it again — often without leaving any trace. Flattening solves this. When you flatten a PDF, the interactive form fields are merged into the page itself, so the answers become part of the document, as fixed as the printed text around them.

This tool flattens a PDF form in one pass, right in your browser. Drop in the file, click once, and download a version where every text field, checkbox and dropdown selection has been baked into the page. The visible content is identical; the difference is that nothing can be typed over anymore. It is the digital equivalent of signing in ink instead of pencil.

Because processing happens locally on your device, the form and everything typed into it stays private — nothing is uploaded. The tool is free, needs no account, applies no watermarks, and handles as many forms as you throw at it.

How to flatten a PDF online

  1. Select or drag your filled PDF form into the upload area. It opens directly in your browser without being sent anywhere.
  2. Wait a moment while the tool reads the form and detects its interactive fields.
  3. Check the field count shown so you know the form was recognized correctly.
  4. Click the flatten button. Every field value is drawn permanently onto the page and the interactive layer is removed.
  5. Download the flattened PDF.
  6. Open it in any reader and try clicking a former field to confirm the responses are locked in.

When to use this tool

  • Lock a signed rental agreement so the tenant's details and dates cannot be altered after both parties have a copy.
  • Flatten completed job application forms before archiving them, ensuring HR records stay exactly as submitted.
  • Prepare an invoice built from a fillable template so the client cannot accidentally edit the totals.
  • Freeze a filled tax or government form before emailing it, since some agency systems reject PDFs with live form fields.
  • Flatten survey responses collected as PDF forms so the data cannot be modified during review.
  • Fix printing problems — some printers skip form field contents entirely, and flattening makes the answers print reliably.

Tips for the best results

  • Keep the original fillable version as a template; flatten a copy each time someone completes it.
  • Flatten before merging forms with other documents, because merge operations can scramble duplicate field names.
  • If a filled form looks empty in someone else's reader, flattening usually fixes it — the values become ordinary page content that every viewer can display.
  • Double-check every field is filled before flattening, since you cannot type into the fields afterwards.
  • Flattening also reduces file size slightly on heavy forms, because the interactive layer is removed.

Frequently asked questions

What does it mean to flatten a PDF?

Flattening merges interactive elements — text fields, checkboxes, dropdowns — into the static page content. The typed answers remain visible exactly where they were, but they become part of the page itself rather than editable fields. The result behaves like a scanned or printed document.

Can a flattened PDF form be edited again?

Not through the form fields, because they no longer exist. Someone with a full PDF editor could still alter page content, as with any PDF, but casual editing of responses is no longer possible. For most contracts and submissions, that is exactly the protection you want.

Will flattening change how the form looks?

No. The tool draws each field's current value onto the page in the same position and font size, so the flattened file looks identical to the filled form. Only the interactivity disappears.

Is my filled form uploaded anywhere?

No. The whole process runs in your browser, so the form — including names, addresses and any sensitive answers typed into it — never leaves your computer. That is a real advantage over server-based converters.

Why do some recipients see my form fields as blank?

Certain viewers, especially on phones and in some email previews, do not render form field values. Flattening converts the answers to regular page content, which every PDF viewer displays correctly.

Does flattening work on signatures and checkboxes too?

Yes. Checkbox states, dropdown selections and drawn or typed signature fields are all merged into the page along with text fields. Everything visible in the filled form is preserved in its flattened state.
