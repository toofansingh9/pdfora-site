PNG is the format to pick when a page is made of edges rather than photographs. Floor plans, circuit diagrams, spreadsheets, maths worksheets and slide decks full of thin lines all suffer under JPEG compression, which leaves grey halos around black strokes. PNG stores every pixel exactly, so a rendered page looks the same in the image as it did on screen in your PDF viewer.

The conversion works page by page. PDF.js, the rendering engine used in Firefox, paints each page onto a canvas, and the canvas is written out as a PNG file. You choose how many pixels that canvas has: Screen draws the page at 1.5 times its size in points (roughly 108 dpi), Standard at 2 times (roughly 144 dpi, the default) and High at 3 times (roughly 216 dpi). Since PNG is lossless, these presets are the only thing that affects quality, and they affect file size a great deal.

The trade-off is weight. A text page saved as PNG is usually compact, because large white areas compress well, but a page carrying a full-colour photograph can become several times larger than the same page as JPG. If your PDF is a scanned document or a photo album, the JPG converter is generally the more practical choice; PNG earns its size on crisp graphics and text.

How to convert PDF pages to PNG

  1. Open PDF to PNG and drop in a single PDF file.
  2. Pick a Quality preset. Standard suits documentation and web use; High is worth it for print, close-up zoom or OCR in another program.
  3. Press Convert and wait while each page is rendered; the bar advances once per page.
  4. Use Download all (.zip) to get every page, or click one of the file buttons to grab a single PNG.
  5. Zoom into the finest detail on one image, such as a dimension label or a footnote, to confirm the preset was high enough.

When to use this tool

  • Putting a technical drawing or wiring diagram into a wiki, ticket or README where thin lines must stay readable.
  • Turning lecture slides exported as PDF into images for a learning platform that accepts only image uploads.
  • Producing clean images of a spreadsheet or table to paste into a report or chat without a grey JPEG fringe around every cell border.
  • Giving a designer high-resolution page images to trace, crop or composite in an image editor.
  • Archiving the exact appearance of a page, such as a signed form, for comparison later, where any compression artefact would be a nuisance.
  • Preparing page images for an OCR program or a translation tool that works better on sharp, artefact-free input.

What to know before you start

  • Pages are drawn on a white background, so the PNG files are not transparent even though the format supports transparency.
  • Every page in the PDF is exported; to convert only a few, cut them out with Extract Pages first.
  • Files are named from the original with a padded page number, such as plans-001.png and plans-002.png, and the ZIP is called plans-png.zip.
  • Selectable text, hyperlinks, bookmarks and form fields do not survive. The PNG shows what the page looked like, nothing more.
  • Pixel size follows the page size stored in the PDF. An A3 drawing at Standard is about 1684 x 2382 pixels; an A4 page about 1191 x 1684.
  • File sizes vary much more than with JPG. Plain text pages can be small; photo-heavy pages or dark backgrounds with noise can run to several megabytes each at High. Dark slides and pages with gradients compress poorly as PNG. If a deck has a black background on every slide, expect large files at High and consider Standard instead.

Tips for the best results

  • For a mixed document, convert twice: PNG for the diagram pages, JPG for the photo pages, then keep whichever file is right for each page.
  • Use High when someone will print the image or read 6-point text from it; Standard is plenty for screen viewing.
  • If the PNGs are too heavy for a website, keep them as PNG but run them through an image optimiser, which can shrink them without losing pixels.
  • Check vector drawings at High. Hairlines that look broken at 108 dpi often render as continuous lines at 216 dpi.
  • Keep the PDF alongside the images; it remains the searchable, scalable master copy. The images are rendered at the moment you convert, so annotations and comments that your viewer shows are included only if PDF.js draws them; hidden layers that are switched off by default stay hidden.

A worked example: a contractor sharing floor plans

A renovation contractor receives an A3 floor plan from an architect as a vector PDF and needs to share room dimensions with a tiler through a messaging app. The tiler opens everything on a phone and does not want to fiddle with PDFs.

Converted to JPG, the dimension labels (small grey text next to thin lines) pick up a blotchy edge that makes 3 and 8 hard to tell apart when zoomed. Converted to PNG at High, the page renders at about 2526 x 3573 pixels and every label stays crisp, because no lossy compression is applied. The file is larger than the JPG, but still easy to send.

The contractor also notices that the architect's plan has thin hatch patterns that disappear at Screen quality. That is not a fault in the plan: at 108 dpi those lines are simply narrower than one pixel. Choosing High fixes it, which is the main reason to have three presets. If the plans had been a scanned paper drawing rather than a vector export, the result would differ: the PNG would reproduce paper grain and grey noise faithfully and the files would be heavy. For scans, JPG at Standard is usually the sensible compromise, and PNG is kept for pages where fine labels must be read.

Troubleshooting

The PNG files are huge
PNG is lossless, so photo-heavy pages are big. Use a lower preset, or convert those pages with PDF to JPG instead.
Thin lines are missing or broken
They are thinner than one pixel at the chosen resolution. Convert again at High.
I expected a transparent background
Pages are rendered over white, so the output is opaque. Remove the background in an image editor if you need transparency.
The tool does not open my PDF
A password or file damage is the usual cause. Remove protection in the original application or try Repair PDF, then convert the fixed copy.
Only 50 download buttons are shown for a long document
The list is capped at 50 buttons for readability. The ZIP download contains every page.
Text looks slightly different from the original
The PDF probably uses a font that is not embedded, and PDF.js substituted a similar one. Export the PDF again with embedded fonts.

Frequently asked questions

Is PNG better than JPG for PDF pages?

For text, charts and line drawings, yes: PNG keeps edges exact. For photographs and scans, JPG usually gives similar visual quality at a much smaller size.

What dpi do the presets correspond to?

Screen is about 108 dpi, Standard about 144 dpi and High about 216 dpi, based on the page size stored in the PDF.

Can the PNGs have a transparent background?

No. The renderer paints the page on white before saving, so every PNG is opaque.

Will I be able to copy text from the PNG?

No, the text becomes pixels. Keep the PDF for copying, or use OCR on the image if the PDF is not available.

Does the conversion need an account or payment?

No. The tool is free to use without signing in.

Why are my PNG files different sizes for pages that look similar?

PNG compresses flat areas very well and noisy areas badly. A page with a photo, gradient or scanned texture produces a much larger file than a page of text on white, even at the same resolution.
