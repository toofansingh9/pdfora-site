Organize Pages

See every page of a PDF as a thumbnail, then drag pages into a new order, rotate or remove them, and save the result as a new file.

Typing page numbers works when you already know them. When you do not, because a scan came out shuffled or a long document needs several fixes at once, you need to see the pages. Organize Pages renders a small thumbnail of every page in the file and puts them on a board where each card can be dragged to a new position, turned left or right, or marked for deletion. Nothing happens to the file until you press Apply & download.

The thumbnails are drawn with PDF.js at roughly a third of the page's normal display size, which is enough to recognise a page by its layout but not to read small print. They are only a preview. When you apply your changes, the tool goes back to the original PDF and uses pdf-lib to copy the real pages, in the board's order, into a new document. Pages you rotated get their rotation setting updated rather than being redrawn, and pages marked for deletion are skipped. The result is saved as yourfile-organized.pdf with text, links to websites and image quality unchanged.

Rendering takes time on long documents because every page is drawn before the board appears. A 20-page letter shows up almost at once; a 400-page scanned book can take a while and will use a lot of memory on a phone. That is the trade-off for being able to see the pages rather than guess at numbers.

How to rearrange PDF pages

  1. Open Organize Pages and choose one PDF. Wait for the message "Rendering pages" to be replaced by the thumbnails.
  2. Drag a page card and drop it where it should go. Each card keeps its original page number as a label, so you can always tell where a page came from.
  3. Use the two arrow buttons on a card to rotate that page 90 degrees left or right; press them more than once for 180 degrees.
  4. Click the bin button to mark a page for deletion. The card is greyed out but stays on the board; click the bin again to restore it.
  5. Press Apply & download and check yourfile-organized.pdf in a viewer before you delete or replace the original.

When to use this tool

  • Putting a school's exam papers back in question order after the scanner jammed and pages were fed in again out of sequence.
  • Fixing a mixed scan where some pages are upside down and some are sideways, and removing the blank sheets, in a single pass.
  • Moving an executive summary from the back of a report to the front before it goes to a committee.
  • Rebuilding a portfolio PDF so the strongest projects come first for a particular client.
  • Tidying a photographed recipe collection or notes file where pages were captured in random order.
  • Checking a document visually page by page before sending it, even if you only end up deleting one stray page.

What to know before you start

  • Only one PDF can be organised at a time. To reorder pages across several files, merge them first with Merge PDF, then organise the merged file.
  • Rotation here changes the page's rotation flag, the same setting a PDF viewer uses when you rotate a page and save. It is lossless, and it adds to any rotation the page already had.
  • Deleted cards stay visible but greyed out until you apply. If every page is marked for deletion, the tool stops with "No pages left to export."
  • Reordering uses the browser's built-in drag and drop. It works with a mouse on desktop browsers; many phone and tablet browsers do not support this kind of dragging, so for heavy reordering a computer is the reliable choice.
  • Because pages are copied into a new document, bookmarks are dropped and links that jump between pages inside the document may break. Fillable form fields may not survive either.
  • There is no undo history. To start over, reload the page and add the file again; your original file has not been changed.

Tips for the best results

  • Do deletions first. Greyed-out cards take up board space, and it is easier to reorder a shorter set mentally even though they remain visible.
  • For long documents, make broad moves first (whole sections to roughly the right place), then fine-tune neighbouring pages.
  • Zoom your browser out to fit more thumbnails on screen; dragging a card past many rows is slower than dropping it on a visible target.
  • If only one or two pages are sideways and nothing else needs to change, the Rotate PDF tool is quicker and keeps bookmarks, because it edits the file instead of rebuilding it.
  • Open the output and step through it once. A misplaced page is far cheaper to catch now than after the file has gone out.

A worked example: a teacher's mis-fed scan

A teacher scans a 14-page practical-science booklet for a student who was absent. The feeder jammed after page 6, so pages 7 to 10 were re-fed and ended up at the end of the scan, after pages 11 to 14. Two sheets went through upside down, and one blank sheet from the bottom of the pile made it in at position 15.

On the board the problem is visible immediately: the cards labelled 7 to 10 sit after 14, and two thumbnails show upside-down text. Dragging the block of four cards into place after page 6 takes four drags. Pressing a rotate button twice on each upside-down card turns it 180 degrees, and the bin on card 15 greys it out.

Apply & download produces a 14-page booklet-organized.pdf in the right order and the right way up. Typing page numbers into separate tools would have taken three passes and some careful arithmetic; here the fix is checked visually as it is made.

Troubleshooting

The board stays on "Rendering pages" for a long time
Every page is drawn before the board appears, so long or image-heavy files take longer. Wait, close other tabs, or split the file into parts, organise each, and merge them again.
I can't drag pages on my phone
Many mobile browsers do not support the drag-and-drop events the board relies on. Use a desktop browser for reordering; rotating and deleting by button still work on touch screens.
A page I rotated is still sideways in the result
Rotation is added to whatever the page already had. If the page was already turned, you may have rotated it into another wrong position. Count the clicks: one is 90 degrees, two is 180.
I get "No pages left to export."
Every card is marked for deletion. Click the bin on at least one card to restore it, then apply again.
My bookmarks are gone
Organize rebuilds the document from copied pages, and bookmarks are not copied. Add them back with the Add Bookmarks tool, or use Rotate PDF if rotation was the only change you needed.
The thumbnails look blank or the file will not load
The PDF may be encrypted or damaged. Try Repair PDF first, or remove the protection in the application that created it or with Unlock PDF if you know the password, then organise the new copy.

Frequently asked questions

Are the thumbnails used in the final file?

No. They are low-resolution previews only. The output is built from the original pages, so quality is identical to the source.

Can I add pages from another PDF on this board?

No, the board shows one file. Merge the files first, then organise the merged PDF.

Can I rotate by 180 degrees?

Yes. Click either rotate button twice. Each click turns the page 90 degrees.

Does organising change my original file?

No. You download a new file named yourfile-organized.pdf; the original on your disk stays as it was.

Is there a page limit?

The tool sets none, but every page is rendered as a thumbnail, so very long files are slow and memory-hungry, especially on phones.

Will scanned pages become searchable?

No, organising only moves, rotates and removes pages. Run OCR PDF on the organised file if you need scanned pages to be searchable.
