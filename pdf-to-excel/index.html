Somewhere in your inbox there is a PDF full of numbers you need in a spreadsheet: a bank statement, a supplier price list, a table of survey results. Copying those cells by hand is slow and error-prone — one misplaced digit in a budget can cost you an afternoon of hunting. PDFora's PDF to Excel converter reads the tables inside your PDF and rebuilds them as an editable XLSX file.

Rows stay rows, columns stay columns, and the values land in real spreadsheet cells you can sort, filter, and run formulas on. The conversion happens on PDFora's secure cloud engine: the file is uploaded over an encrypted connection, processed, and automatically deleted the moment the job is done. Unlike most PDFora tools, which run entirely in your browser, PDF to XLSX conversion needs server-side muscle — but the privacy rules are just as strict.

You will not hit a paywall halfway through. The tool is free, asks for no signup, and never brands your spreadsheet with a watermark. Upload the PDF, download the Excel file, and start working with your data instead of retyping it. For scanned tables, run OCR first so there is real text for the converter to extract.

How to convert PDF to Excel online

  1. Go to the PDF to Excel tool on getpdfora.com.
  2. Drag your PDF onto the upload area, or click to browse and select it.
  3. Let the secure conversion engine detect the tables and rebuild them as spreadsheet cells — this usually takes only a few seconds.
  4. Download the finished XLSX file to your device.
  5. Open it in Excel, Google Sheets, or LibreOffice Calc.
  6. Scan the header rows and a few sample values to confirm everything landed in the right columns before you build formulas on top.

When to use this tool

  • Pull transactions from a PDF bank or credit card statement into Excel for monthly budgeting or expense reports.
  • Convert a supplier's PDF price list into a spreadsheet so you can compare vendors side by side.
  • Extract financial tables from an annual report to build your own analysis and charts.
  • Move exported sales or inventory data — delivered as PDF by an old system — back into a workable XLSX format.
  • Digitize utility bills and invoices into one running spreadsheet at tax time instead of typing totals by hand.
  • Turn published statistics from a government or research PDF into sortable data for a paper or presentation.

Tips for the best results

  • PDFs with clear, gridded tables convert most accurately; tables that rely on whitespace alone can occasionally merge or split columns, so give those a quick review.
  • If your source is a scanned or photographed document, run OCR before converting — the extractor needs real text, not an image of numbers.
  • Big report? Extract just the pages containing tables first. You will get a cleaner spreadsheet without pages of narrative text mixed in.
  • Numbers formatted as currency or percentages arrive as text in some cases; Excel's Convert to Number prompt fixes an entire column in one click.
  • Spot-check totals. Summing a converted column and comparing it to the PDF's printed total is the fastest way to confirm nothing slipped.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the PDF to Excel conversion?

Well-structured tables with visible borders usually convert cleanly, with each value in its own cell. Loosely formatted tables or ones spanning many pages may need a few columns adjusted afterwards. It is still dramatically faster than retyping.

Can I convert a scanned PDF table to Excel?

Not without a first step. Scanned pages are images, so the converter has no text to extract. Run OCR on the scan, then convert the result to XLSX.

Does this tool cost anything or require an account?

No. It is completely free, with no signup, no watermarks, and no limit tricks. Convert as many statements and reports as you need.

Is it safe to upload financial documents?

The conversion runs on PDFora's secure cloud engine over an encrypted connection, and your file is automatically deleted after processing. It is never stored, indexed, or shared with anyone.

Will formulas from the original spreadsheet come back?

No — a PDF only stores the printed values, not the formulas behind them. The converter recovers the numbers and layout; you can rebuild calculations in Excel afterwards.

What if my PDF has several tables on different pages?

The engine processes the whole document and extracts each table it finds. Review the output sheet by sheet, since tables that continue across page breaks are sometimes split and easy to rejoin.
